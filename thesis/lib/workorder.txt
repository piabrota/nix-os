## common
YOU WRITE ONE SECTION. Nothing else. You do not read the text of any other section.
The facts of the thesis are in notes/facts.md and the terms in notes/glossary.md: read both first.
If a fact you need is not there or in a verified source, do NOT write it. Put the question in
notes/@ID@.questions.md and write the rest. Never invent a number, a result, a reference or an
experience of the author. Where only the author knows (a decision, an anecdote), leave nothing:
ask in notes/@ID@.questions.md. (The markers **[AUTHOR: ...]** and **[ANALYSIS]** are for the editor
and the author: a section that still has one is refused by `thesis submit`.)
Write in pt-BR, as a person who knows the subject: plain academic Portuguese. Pandoc markdown.
Tools: you may run `thesis lint`, `slop`, `readability`, `cite-check`, `cite-score`, `build-section`
on your section as often as you like before you hand in.

## style
Human readable (gate 1):
- Sentences of about 15 to 25 words; none over 40. Vary the length. One idea a sentence.
- Paragraphs of 3 to 6 sentences, one topic each. No one-sentence paragraphs.
- Active voice. Say who does what: "O código calcula...", not "É calculado...".
- One word for one thing, the same all the time (see notes/glossary.md). Use the Portuguese term; an
  English term only if the glossary lists it. Define each acronym once, the first time.
- Numbers, not adjectives: "reduziu o tempo de 42 s para 6 s", not "reduziu muito o tempo".
- Do not number headings by hand: LaTeX numbers chapters and sections. Use `# Heading` for a section of
  the chapter and `##` for a subsection. Do not write the chapter title as a heading.
Low AI marks (gate 2): rewrite from the facts; do not paraphrase a draft. Avoid:
- stock phrases: "vale ressaltar", "é importante destacar", "no cenário atual", "nesse sentido",
  "em suma", "cabe salientar", "panorama", "robusto", "holístico", "desempenha um papel fundamental",
  "ao longo dos anos", "diversos", "um leque de" (the full list is in `thesis slop`);
- em dashes and spaced en dashes; use commas, colons or full stops;
- three parallel parts in a row ("A, B e C") over and over; the same opening word on three
  sentences in a row; a summary sentence at the end of every paragraph; a transition word
  ("Além disso", "Dessa forma") at the start of every paragraph; bold lead-ins in lists;
- a first sentence that repeats the chapter title.
Be specific: name the model, the version, the dataset, the number, the date.
Citations (gate 4):
- Cite only the sources listed below as VERIFIED, as [@key] (or @key in the sentence: "Tolman (2009)").
  Never type (Author, 2016) by hand. Never cite a key that is not in the list.
- Every claim that comes from a work carries its mark. Say what the work says, and the page for a
  direct quote ([@key, p. 12]). A quotation of three lines or more goes in a block: `> text`.
- Your own results and decisions need no citation. Mark nothing as yours that you did not do.
- A figure is `![Caption](figures/name.pdf){#fig:name}`; a table has a line under it:
  `: Caption {#tbl:name}`; an equation is `$$ ... $$ {#eq:name}`. Cite each in the text:
  `@fig:name`, `@tbl:name`, `@eq:name`. Write a caption as a phrase, with no full stop at the end.
  Table caption goes above, figure caption below: the build does that. Only math on the allow list is
  accepted; raw TeX and HTML are switched off and print as text.
- To refer to a whole chapter write `@cap:<section id>`. Do not refer to another section's figures.

## role:chapter
UFRJ rules for a textual chapter (Res. 05/2012, textual part; norma 2026):
- The chapter holds its part of the work: Introdução; Revisão da Literatura; Metodologia;
  Resultados; Discussão; Conclusões (a chapter may group them).
- Text in black. Figures and tables numbered and cited in the text. Credit every idea, table and
  figure of other people, even from the Internet.
- Do not write "Capítulo N" yourself: use @cap:<id>. Do not number your headings.
- Keep facts and analysis in separate paragraphs.

## role:resumo
UFRJ rules for the RESUMO (Res. 05/2012; norma 2026, 7): one page, one paragraph, up to 250 words,
no citations, no headings, no list, no table, no figure. Write it from the facts: the problem, what
was done, how it was measured, the main result with its number, and what it means. The 3 to 6
keywords go in thesis.json, in the field `keywords` of this section (the editor sets them; say in
notes/@ID@.questions.md which words you propose).

## role:abstract
UFRJ rules for the ABSTRACT (Res. 05/2012; norma 2026, 7): English, one page, one paragraph, up to
250 words, no citations, the same facts as the resumo and nothing new. Plain academic English; the
gate-2 advice applies to English too. The 3 to 6 keywords go in thesis.json.

## role:agradecimentos
Optional sheet. Write only what notes/facts.md says. A short text. No citations.

## role:dedicatoria
Optional sheet. A short text the author gave in notes/facts.md. No citations.

## role:appendix
Appendices hold detail that the text refers to: listings, long tables, derivations. Each is a chapter
of letters (A, B, ...). Cite the appendix from the text with @cap:<id>.

## librarian
You do not write sections. You make the sources: for each key a section needs, fetch the work,
record the check, and only then add it to refs.bib. Rules (closed-source): never invent a
reference; fetch every source before citing it; if you cannot fetch it, do not add it. Write
sources/<key>.md with these lines:
  URL: https://...
  Retrieved: YYYY-MM-DD
  DOI check: 10.xxxx/... resolves to this title and these authors   (or `none`)
  > "the exact passage, copied" (p. N)
Then a row in REFERENCES.md: | Key | Status | Checked against | Note |, with status verified (fetched,
details match), partial (fetched, one detail unconfirmed: leave that field out of refs.bib and write
`unconfirmed: field` in the Note) or unverified (not fetched; must not be cited). Fields NBR 6023 needs
for the type of work, a DOI as 10.xxxx/yyyy (no https://doi.org/), and a url with urldate.
